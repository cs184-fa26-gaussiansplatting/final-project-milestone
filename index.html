Summary / What We Have Accomplished

We built the pre-training half of the mesh-to-gsplat pipeline and ran it end-to-end on one test mesh. src/sample.py area-weighted barycentric-samples an .obj (+ sidecar .mtl) into a 10k-point cloud (output/points.npy, output/points.ply preview). blender/setup_scene.py imports the mesh, bundles an HDRI, and renders 8 reference images from an orb of cameras (output/renders/camXX.png + output/cameras.json). src/convert.py converts the point cloud into an initial output/splat.ply (mean, SH-DC color, scale 0.005, quaternion from normal, opacity 0.95) in the gsplat/SuperSplat binary layout. Timing is recorded for load/sample/save and conversion.

Preliminary Results

  • 10,000 points sampled from data/test.obj; degenerate faces skipped.
  • 8 reference renders captured from evenly spaced orb cameras pointing at the model center.
  • output/splat.ply written (10k Gaussians) and opens in SuperSplat for inspection.
  • Sampling completes in well under a second; conversion is near-instant, so the milestone timing target (fast .obj→.ply pre-training output) is met.

Progress Reflection vs. Plan

Per the milestone plan we scoped out sampling, conversion, Blender rendering, an end-to-end run, the webpage, video, and slides — and deliberately deferred full training, Adaptive Density Control, adaptive cameras, and the final report. All in-scope milestone items are complete and the pipeline produces a viewable .ply. Render cost stayed bounded by limiting coverage to 8 cameras, addressing the identified risk. Remaining work: training loop, densification/optimization, and final deliverables (video, slides, PDF).
